How it works

What it does

Forever Sim plays your character through the same fights thousands of times. Every swing, spell, critical hit, dodge and proc is a dice roll, just as in the game, so no two fights go quite the same way. Averaging them shows what a build really does.

Set up one to three builds, press Simulate, and each build plays through every test: leveling against the open world’s mix of beasts, humanoids (some of them casters), undead and more, one, two or three at a time; two tough mobs at once; a dungeon boss with a tank and a party’s buffs; two world PvP ambushes; and duels between your builds. Healers also heal a group: a dungeon, a main tank and, at level 60, a raid. Everything runs on your own computer; nothing is uploaded.

Two more tabs run bigger jobs. Class rankings puts every build on the site through seven tests, and the healers through the healing tests, and ranks them. Duels pits one build against every other: both start a few yards apart on the same tick, and whoever reacts first strikes first.

Reading the results

  • Each test has a row with every build’s result. Open it for the details: a summary, how the build played, one fight step by step, and every number.
  • The main build wears the gold dragon ring. The others are measured against it, and its fights are the ones shown first. Any build can be the main build; use its card’s menu.
  • “About the same” means the gap is smaller than the simulation’s margin of error. Every build faces exactly the same dice rolls, so a clear gap comes from the builds, not luck.
  • Ranges are 95% confidence intervals: if you ran the test again, the average would almost always land inside them.

Trying a change

Press “Add a build to compare” to copy a build, change something in the copy, and simulate. After you change a build and simulate again, the results say what your change did, measured on the same fights as before.

What it cannot tell you

Talents marked with a grey corner are not simulated yet and change nothing. Everyone moves along one straight line, so there’s no terrain, line of sight or pathing. A few numbers the game does not publish are guesses; you can change them under Assumptions. The simulated player plays like a typical player: it takes about half a second to react and slips now and then. Switch it to Expert under Assumptions to see what a build does at its best.
